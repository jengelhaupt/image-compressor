Shrinking JPG files – when does it make sense?

JPG is mainly used for photos because it offers a good balance between quality and file size. Even so, JPG files can quickly grow to several megabytes.

Compression is especially useful when:

  • images are to be sent by email
  • photos are needed for online job applications
  • many images are to be archived or stored
  • uploads to websites should be faster

Depending on the setting, you can save JPG files either nearly lossless or with stronger compression to get the smallest possible files.

Optimizing PNG files – uses and benefits

PNG is often used for graphics where high quality or transparency matters, such as logos, icons or screenshots.

Optimization is particularly worthwhile for:

  • Graphics and logos that need to look sharp
  • images with a transparent background
  • screenshots or UI elements
  • use on websites, to reduce loading times

Because PNG is lossless, image quality is usually fully preserved during compression.

Understanding WebP files and using them wisely

WebP is a modern image format developed specifically for the web. It combines small file sizes with good image quality and also supports transparency.

WebP is especially suitable:

  • for websites and online projects
  • when loading times should be optimized
  • as an alternative to JPG and PNG
  • for images that should be stored at the smallest possible size

With additional compression you can reduce the file size further without noticeably affecting image quality.

Shrinking PDF files – when does it make sense?

PDF files are often used for documents such as job applications, invoices or presentations. Depending on the content, they can quickly become very large – especially when they contain many images.

Compression makes sense when:

  • PDFs are to be sent by email
  • upload limits for applications or forms must be met
  • documents are shared online or uploaded
  • storage space should be saved or files archived

Compression mainly optimizes embedded images and removes unnecessary data. Depending on the setting, you can achieve a smaller file size without noticeably affecting the readability or quality of the document.
