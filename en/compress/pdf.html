How PDF compression works

During PDF compression, images inside the PDF are recalculated and reduced to a defined DPI resolution. Depending on the level you choose, how text is handled also changes:

  • High levels keep text as text
  • Stronger compression converts pages to images to achieve maximum file reduction

That gives you full control over quality, readability and file size.

Compression levels and DPI explained

PDF compression works with fixed levels:

  • Quality 90%: Images: 300 DPI | Text stays text
  • Quality 80%: Images: 200 DPI | Text is rasterized to an image
  • Quality 70%: Images: 150 DPI | Text is rasterized to an image
  • Quality 60%: Images: 120 DPI | Text is rasterized to an image
  • Quality 50%: Images: 100 DPI | Text is rasterized to an image
  • Quality 40%: Images: 90 DPI | Text is rasterized to an image
  • Quality 30%: Images: 72 DPI | Text is rasterized to an image

The lower the DPI, the smaller the PDF file. For printing a high DPI is recommended; for screen, email or web, lower levels are enough.

Which level for which use?

  • 90% (300 DPI): Ideal for printing, archiving and high-quality documents
  • 80–70% (200–150 DPI): Good balance between quality and file size
  • 60–50% (120–100 DPI): Optimal for email, uploads and internal use
  • 40–30% (90–72 DPI): Maximum compression for web, previews and fast transfer
